Work through it on your own first, as if it were the exam, then compare with a neighbour or ask a TA.

(Use any remaining time to catch up on earlier practicals and weekly quizzes, and to look at the mock exam.)

The study

Does climate warming change how plants respond to nitrogen deposition? To find out, researchers used 12 growth chambers. Six chambers were kept at ambient temperature and six were warmed by 3 °C; the temperature treatment was randomly assigned to chambers. In each chamber there were 8 pots, each with one plant: 4 pots received low nitrogen and 4 received high nitrogen (randomly assigned to pots within each chamber). After 8 weeks, the above-ground biomass of each plant was measured (g).

Question: does the effect of nitrogen on plant biomass depend on warming?

(The data are simulated for teaching purposes.)

The dataset chamber_warming.csv has one row per pot, with the variables chamber, warming (ambient or warmed), pot, nitrogen (low or high) and biomass_g.

1. Plan before you look at the data

2. Import, check and visualise

library(tidyverse)
library(lme4)
library(lmerTest)
chamber_warming <- read_csv("https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opetchey/BIO144_Practicals_WAs/refs/heads/main/assets/datasets/chamber_warming.csv")
chamber_warming <- chamber_warming |>
  mutate(nitrogen = fct_relevel(nitrogen, "low"),
         warming = factor(warming))

Check the number of rows, the number of chambers, and the number of pots per treatment combination. Then make a graph that shows the answer to the question: biomass for each combination of warming and nitrogen, with the points of each chamber identifiable (e.g. colour by chamber, or facet by chamber).

3. Fit the models

First fit the model that ignores the chambers, so you can see what goes wrong; then the mixed model.

m_biomass_lm <- lm(biomass_g ~ warming * nitrogen, data = chamber_warming)
m_biomass_mixed <- lmer(biomass_g ~ warming * nitrogen + (1 | chamber), data = chamber_warming)
summary(m_biomass_lm)
summary(m_biomass_mixed)
anova(m_biomass_mixed)

4. Check the model

Plot the residuals against the fitted values (plot(m_biomass_mixed); autoplot() does not work for mixed models), make a QQ-plot of the residuals (qqnorm(resid(m_biomass_mixed))), and look at the estimated chamber effects (ranef(m_biomass_mixed)). Are there any obvious problems?

5. Answer the question

Now make a figure for publication: the data (e.g. chamber means, or all pots with some transparency), together with the model’s predicted mean for each treatment combination and its confidence interval. Use predict(m_biomass_mixed, newdata = ..., re.form = NA) to get predictions for an average chamber.

6. Report

Reflection. How would you redesign this experiment if you were mainly interested in the effect of warming? (Hint: what limits the precision of the warming effect: the number of pots, or the number of chambers?)
